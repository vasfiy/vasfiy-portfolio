Quotes & idioms

Lines to remember and reuse when you speak.

“Yesterday is history, tomorrow is a mystery, but today is a gift. That's why it's called the present.”

Wordplay: present means both “now” and “a gift”.

“Nobody remembers the ordinary days of childhood — but they are the ones that made you.”

The everyday moments we forget are the ones that shaped who we are.

“He always makes us laugh.”

The easiest way to describe the funny one in your family. make + somebody + verb: makes me laugh, makes us smile. Uz: U bizni doim kuldiradi.

“I take after my mother, but I look like my father.”

take after = similar in character; look like = similar in appearance. Uz: Fe'lim onamga tortgan, lekin tashqi ko'rinishim otamga o'xshaydi.

“We just talk about this and that.”

Written on the board as talk (from here and there): chatting about many small, random topics. Uz: u yoq-bu yoqdan gaplashmoq.

Lesson 2 · Talking About Family · Pronunciation

Talking about your family, the relaxed way

Start with a filler (Well…, You know…), then say how many you are, who is who, what they are like and what you do together. Every underlined group shows a sound change from the board.

Standard“Well, you know, my family is pretty big. There are six of us. I'm the oldest. My father is strict and quiet, my mother is kind, and my brother is the funny one. He always makes us laugh…”
Real speak“Well, y'know, my family's priddy big. There're six of us — my grandparents, my parents, my little brother an' me. A'm d oldest. My father's schrict an' kwayt, an' my mother's kind an' funny. My brother's the funny one — he always makes us laf. He's tokadiv too. On weekends we go ta the mauntns or go fora wok, an' in the evenin' we wotch series t'gezzer an' jrink tea. Sometimes we argyu about football, but we get along really well.”

Lesson 9 · A Childhood Memory · Pronunciation

Dense casual American pronunciation

Read it fast and relaxed, as if you were talking to a close friend. Every underlined group is a reduction: squeeze it — don't use the full, careful form.

Standard“I grew up in a small village with my grandmother, and I used to play outside from morning until dark…”
Real speak“A grew up in a small village with my gran'mother. We didn' have much — no computer, no phone — but a don' remember bein' bored once. A used ta play outside from mornin' till dark, climbin' trees, runnin' aroun' with the neighbors' kids. One summer a broke her kitchen window with a ball. A wuz so afraid a hid in the garden fer two hours. She found me, an' she jus' laughed. A'll never ferget that. Lookin' back, it wuz a good childhood. A really miss id sometimes.”
